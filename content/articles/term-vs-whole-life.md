# Term Life and Whole of Life: Understanding the Difference

Compare the purpose, duration and policy structure before focusing on a product name.

## Begin with the purpose of protection
Life insurance decisions are easier to discuss when the objective is clear. You may be considering a period of borrowing, dependants who rely on your income, or longer-term family responsibilities. The amount and duration of the need are important starting points.

Term life and whole-of-life structures address duration differently, but a product label alone does not tell you whether an arrangement fits. Premiums, benefits, exclusions and other conditions need to be read together.

## How the broad structures differ
Term life is generally structured to provide life cover for a specified period under the policy. Whole-of-life arrangements are designed for longer-duration cover, subject to their terms, premiums and other requirements. Neither description removes the need to review the actual policy.

| Question | Term life discussion | Whole-of-life discussion |
|---|---|---|
| Duration | What period does the policy cover? | What conditions allow cover to continue? |
| Premiums | Are premiums fixed or capable of changing? | What payments are required over time? |
| Benefits | What events and amounts are insured? | What benefits and any value features apply? |
| Ending the policy | What happens at expiry or early cancellation? | What happens on surrender, lapse or cancellation? |

These are comparison questions rather than statements that every product has the same features. Ask for explanations using the documents for the products under consideration.

## Think about affordability over time
An arrangement should be considered in light of the ability to maintain the required premiums. Look beyond the first payment and ask how the premium pattern may change, what happens if payments stop and which options exist under the policy.

Where a product includes illustrated or non-guaranteed values, distinguish those from contractual guarantees. Do not treat an illustration as a promise of a future result. Ask which assumptions are being used and which benefits are guaranteed by the wording.

## Keep underwriting and replacement in view
Health information, eligibility and underwriting can affect the terms offered. Give accurate information through the appropriate process and ask how the decision relates to any existing cover.

Changing a policy may have consequences such as new underwriting, different exclusions or loss of existing features. Do not cancel a policy simply because an alternative has been discussed; understand whether new cover has actually been accepted and commenced, and what ending the old policy would mean.

## Questions to bring to the conversation
- Who or what is the protection intended to support?
- How much cover and what duration should be considered?
- Can the required premiums be maintained?
- Which benefits are guaranteed and which are not?
- What would changing or ending an existing policy involve?
