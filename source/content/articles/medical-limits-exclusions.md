# Understanding Medical Insurance Limits and Exclusions

Look beyond a headline benefit amount to understand how medical cover may operate.

## The headline limit is only one part of cover
Medical insurance policies may specify an overall limit alongside separate limits for particular treatment or services. A large headline figure therefore does not mean every eligible expense is reimbursed without restriction.

Read the benefit table alongside the wording and endorsements. Ask how limits interact and whether they apply per year, event, treatment or another basis. The answer depends on the selected policy.

## Understand your contribution
A deductible, excess or co-payment can affect the amount you bear yourself. Different policies may calculate contributions differently. Ask for an explanation using a simple hypothetical bill, while recognising that a real claim depends on eligibility and the actual circumstances.

Compare cost-sharing alongside premium. A lower premium may accompany a different contribution structure, but the practical comparison must use the particular products under consideration rather than a general assumption.

## Review location and provider rules
Treatment geography, provider networks and reimbursement arrangements can matter. Check whether a preferred practitioner or hospital is relevant to the plan and whether treatment outside a network changes the benefit.

Direct billing and pre-authorisation arrangements, where available, do not remove the need to understand the policy. Ask which services require advance contact and which documents or steps are needed. Do not assume a hospital’s willingness to treat you confirms insurance cover.

## Ask about exclusions and waiting periods
An exclusion identifies a circumstance or expense outside the relevant cover. A waiting period can affect when a benefit becomes available. Existing medical conditions and underwriting may also influence the terms offered.

Disclose information accurately through the insurer’s or broker’s appropriate process. Ask for clarity about any exclusion or special term before relying on the policy. A general website statement cannot confirm how your medical circumstances will be treated.

## Keep dental benefits separate in the comparison
Dental treatment is not automatically included in medical cover. Where dental benefits are offered, check the eligible procedures, annual limits, waiting periods and any contribution required. Routine treatment and more complex procedures may be treated differently under the wording.

## Questions for a useful review
- What overall limits and sub-limits apply?
- What costs must I contribute and how are they calculated?
- Which treatment locations and providers can I use?
- What exclusions, waiting periods or underwriting terms apply?
- Are dental benefits included or arranged separately?
- Which authorisation or notification steps are required?

Keep sensitive medical records out of a public enquiry form. Start with the type of cover you want to discuss and request an appropriate channel if detailed information is needed.
